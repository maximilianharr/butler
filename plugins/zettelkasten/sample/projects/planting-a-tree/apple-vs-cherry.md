# Apple Tree 

## Fruit
Offers a wide variety of flavors, from sweet to tart. Apples are versatile, perfect for eating fresh, cooking, and storing for the winter.   Harvest times range from summer to late fall. 	

## Insects and Birds
The blossoms provide nectar for pollinators in the spring, and fallen apples are a valuable food source for birds and small mammals in the autumn.

## Planting and Maintenance
Apple trees require full sun and well-drained soil. Most varieties need another apple or crabapple tree nearby for cross-pollination. Annual pruning is necessary, and it's wise to choose disease-resistant varieties to minimize issues with pests and diseases like apple scab and fire blight. 

# Cherry Tree

## Fruit 
You can choose between sweet cherries for fresh eating or sour cherries, ideal for pies and preserves.   Be prepared to share with birds, as they are very fond of the fruit; you may need to use netting to protect your harvest.   

## Insects and Birds
Cherry blossoms are an early and important source of nectar for pollinators. The fruits are a favorite of many bird species.   

## Planting and Maintenance
Cherry trees also thrive in full sun and prefer well-drained, sandy soil. While sour cherries are often self-pollinating, most sweet cherry varieties need a second tree for pollination. They can be high-maintenance and are susceptible to pests like the cherry fruit fly. For Central Europe, sour cherries are generally a hardier option than sweet cherries.
